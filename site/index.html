Independent TESS transit searches

Faint transits in light TESS had already recorded

Eumemic·github.com/eumemic/exoplanets·Code MIT · results CC BY 4.0

Independent searches of NASA TESS light curves for small transiting planets. Each star's sectors are combined, so a dip too faint to notice in one month of data can stand out across years of it. A full-sky search of 4.04 million FGKM dwarfs, and earlier searches of nearby K and M dwarfs and of the hosts of TESS Objects of Interest, give … candidate signals that pass automated and pixel-level vetting. Most are weak in any single sector and sit on stars observed only in full-frame images, which is why earlier searches did not report them. They are candidates, not confirmed planets: ground-based photometry, high-resolution imaging or radial velocities are needed to confirm or reject each one.

Simulated white noise with the depth (1,142 ppm) and duration (2.5 h) of the TOI-4342 candidate, scaled so one sector gives SNR 3. Twelve sectors give about SNR 10. SPOC's single-sector threshold is 7.1. Real light curves also contain red noise.

01 // How a transit worksephemerides.csv

A planet's shadow is a dip in starlight

When a planet crosses the face of its star it blocks a fraction of the light close to the ratio of the two discs' areas, so the depth of the dip is about (Rp/R*)². The star is brighter at its centre than at its edge (limb darkening), which rounds the bottom of the dip. A real candidate, drawn to scale:

02 // The search, as a funnelREADMECSV

From four million stars to a few hundred signals

The full-sky search looked at every FGKM dwarf with T ≤ 13.5 that the earlier searches had not covered, combining all of its TESS sectors (1–104). Almost every signal it finds is noise, a known planet or an eclipsing binary. Each stage below removes most of what is left.

    04 // What they areCSV

    Size against orbit

    Orbital period against fitted radius for every counted candidate. Two regions of this plane are known to be sparse in planets: the hot Neptunian desert, where few Neptune-sized planets survive close to their stars, and the radius valley between rocky super-Earths and gas-rich sub-Neptunes. Both are drawn as approximate regions.

    Desert core: P < 3.2 d and 4–10 R⊕ (Castro-González et al. 2024), the definition of the catalogue's desert_flag. Radius valley: the band log₁₀R = −0.09 log₁₀P + 0.37 ± 0.045 (Van Eylen et al. 2018), measured for Sun-like stars. It is shown for orientation and is not used by the pipeline. Radii use TIC 8.2 stellar radii.

    05 // Hidden in plain sightfindability.csv

    The data are not new

    For each full-sky candidate in tiers A, B and N, the transits observed by the end of a given sector were combined to ask when it could first have been found. Many were already at SNR 9 years ago. They were missed because they are weak in any one sector, and because most are on stars observed only in full-frame images, which SPOC's multi-sector searches do not cover.

    Candidates at SNR > 9, by data through sector

    From validation/findability.md and findability.csv (12 October 2026). Each SNR is the inverse-variance mean depth of the transits observed by then, over its error; this ignores how fewer sectors would change the detrending and the period search. The light curves used here lacked many QLP products from Sectors 94–97 and 101–104, so the contribution of the newest sectors is understated.

    06 // The vetting gauntletREADME & validation notes

    Every signal runs the same checks

    Most transit-like signals in full-frame images are not planets around the target: they are noise, systematics, or eclipsing binaries whose light is blended with the target's. These are the checks a counted candidate has passed, in order.

    2. LEO-Vetter

      all searches
      13,986 of 609,021 pre-filtered full-sky signals pass every test

      The 13 false-alarm and 4 false-positive tests of LEO-Vetter (Kunimoto et al. 2025), run on unmatched signals after transit-masked re-detrending. Method, step 7

    3. Per-sector depth consistency

      all searches
      9,962 remain with MES ≥ 7.1 and χ²/dof ≤ 5

      The transit depth must agree from sector to sector, as it would for a planet on the target. Full-sky funnel

    4. Centroids in every sector

      12 Oct 2026
      rejects 0 of 565 known planets and 73% of 175 neighbour signals at p < 0.001

      The difference-image centroid is measured in every sector with transits and tested for a consistent offset away from the target. 18 counted candidates rejected their target and left the counted tiers. centroid_sectors.md

    5. Nearby-star checks

      all searches
      726 SNR ≥ 9 signals have no centroid offset and no capable neighbour within 15″

      TIC stars within 2.5′ that could produce the depth as an eclipsing binary. Signals whose only problem is such a star inside 15″ go on to TRICERATOPS and a centroid likelihood ratio, and can enter tier N (MES ≥ 10). Second release

    6. Binary catalogues

      from 8 Oct 2026
      … full-sky signals are listed as likely binaries

      Gaia DR3 (RUWE, eclipsing binaries, non-single-star orbits), the TESS eclipsing-binary catalogue (Prša et al. 2022), APOGEE binaries (Kounkel et al. 2021) and VSX. Grazing transits (b ≥ 0.9) and RUWE > 1.4 go to tier C, which is not counted. Reliability tiers

    7. TRICERATOPS with Gaia limits

      11 Oct 2026
      median FPP 0.042 → 0.013 with Gaia limits; … counted candidates now meet FPP < 0.015 and NFPP < 0.001

      Every counted candidate must have FPP < 0.5 and NFPP < 0.1 after the centroid Bayes factor. MOLUSC removes bound companions that Gaia would have resolved, and Gaia epoch RVs rule out an eclipsing binary on bright targets. Statistical validation would also need high-resolution imaging. gaia_companion_limits.md

    8. False-alarm classifier

      revised 13 Oct 2026
      selects tier L1 from the SNR 7.5–9 band: … counted

      A gradient-boosted classifier trained to tell the real search's signals from those found in inverted light curves, where every dip is noise. Since 13 October its validation groups signals by star: 8 tier L1 candidates fell below the 0.80 cut and 11 joined. false_alarm_classifier.md

    9. TIC artifacts removed

      13 Oct 2026
      30 ARTIFACT and 68 DUPLICATE entries among 3,454 capable neighbours

      TIC entries that are not stars (Paegert et al. 2021) no longer count as possible sources of a dip. 7 tier N candidates became tier B, and two earlier-search signals joined, including a 27.0 d signal around WASP-166. tic_artifacts.md

    Quoted numbers are from the README and the linked notes, dated as tagged. Live numbers are computed from the candidate tables.

    07 // SpotlightCandidateREADME · 7 Oct 2026

    TOI-4342: a possible third transiting planet

    orbit sizes to scale · phases illustrative
    • TOI-4342 b and c are confirmed sub-Neptunes at 5.54 and 10.69 d (Tey et al. 2023; masses from ESPRESSO, 2026, which also reports a probably non-transiting 47.5 d RV candidate).
    • The candidate transits every 18.9082 d with the same radius as b and c (2.2 R⊕), in 8 sectors: 11σ in Sectors 13–93 and 7σ in 101–103.
    • The difference-image centroid is within 7–16″ of the star in each 2-minute sector, and no star within 30″ is bright enough to produce the dip.
    • Its predicted radial-velocity semi-amplitude is about 2 m/s. The TOI-host search, which masks b and c first, finds it again as the strongest remaining signal.

    09 // Open questions and how to helpREADME · 13 Oct 2026

    What is not yet known

    Needs observers

    Confirm or reject each candidate

    TESS alone cannot rule out every false-positive scenario. Ground-based photometry at the predicted times, high-resolution imaging and radial velocities are needed. The README expects false-positive rates of tens of percent in tier B, lower in tier A.

    Blind spot

    Companions closer than 1–2″

    The remaining blind spot of the pixel checks is a companion star closer than about 1–2″, which only high-resolution imaging can rule out.

    Re-search planned

    About 900,000 missing QLP light curves

    The reader dropped QLP light curves whose uncertainty column is empty (PR #6, merged): 24–82% of those in Sectors 94–97 and 101–104 in the earlier searches. A re-search of the affected full-sky stars is planned.

    Waiting on MAST

    A QLP timing error

    MAST reports a QLP timing error from the second half of Sector 99 to Sector 104, and the corrected files are not yet delivered. Fits that use those sectors may shift by minutes.

    Expected

    Some false alarms remain

    From the inverted-light-curve measurements, about 10 false alarms are expected among the tier N candidates, and about 92% of the SNR 7.5–9 signals the classifier selects are expected to be astrophysical (released estimate).

    How to help

    Observe, check, contribute

    Pick a transit in the planner, look through a candidate's vetting plot in the explorer, or open an issue or pull request on GitHub. Two of the latest corrections came from an outside contributor.
